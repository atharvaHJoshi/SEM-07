ISRO has successfully launched its latest communication satellite into orbit from the Sriharikota space centre. The satellite, named GSAT-25, was carried by the heaviest rocket built by ISRO. The launch took place early on Friday morning and thousands of people watched it live from the viewing gallery. Scientists at the mission control centre celebrated after the satellite was placed in the correct orbit.

The GSAT-25 satellite will provide high speed internet and television services across the country. It weighs about four thousand kilograms and has a mission life of fifteen years. The satellite's solar panels were deployed shortly after it reached orbit, and all systems are working normally. The ISRO chairman said the successful launch of the satellite is an important step for the country's space programme.

The new satellite will improve internet connectivity in remote villages and islands. ISRO is also preparing for its next launch, which will carry weather monitoring instruments. The data from GSAT-25 will support telemedicine and online education services. The space agency plans to launch several more communication satellites in the coming years, and the team is already working on those missions.
